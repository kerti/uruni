# ADR-042 - Reversing any ordinary transaction

**Status:** Proposed · `draft` - drafted 2026-10-20 ([#629](https://github.com/kerti/uruni/issues/629), epic [#523](https://github.com/kerti/uruni/issues/523)), grilled and approved by the maintainer 2026-10-20; a draft with [#630](https://github.com/kerti/uruni/issues/630)'s code behind it (an edit ships in the PR with the code that makes it true), still editable until [#631](https://github.com/kerti/uruni/issues/631) lands · partly supersedes [ADR-029](./029-reversing-a-dues-payment.md), [ADR-033](./033-correcting-a-posted-purpose.md), [ADR-034](./034-envelope-participation.md) and [ADR-038](./038-the-month-as-a-balance-walk.md), each clause named at the foot · keeps [ADR-031](./031-posting-to-a-closed-incidental.md) · [ADR index](./README.md)

**Context.** A treasurer on a phone taps the wrong direction: a coupon received is recorded as money out ([#618](https://github.com/kerti/uruni/issues/618)). Transactions are immutable (CLAUDE.md rule 3), so the fix today is a second `normal` row going the other way, then the right one. The balance comes out right and everything beside it comes out wrong. ADR-038's walk counts the mistake in *Total keluar* and the hand-made fix in *Total masuk*, so the month is inflated on both sides, and nothing links the two rows: a neighbour reading the public report sees money received and money spent.

The ledger already reverses a row with a linked entry, and refuses to for this one. `reverses_transaction_id` (ADR-029, widened to a named contribution by ADR-034) may sit only on a `kind='adjustment'`, `direction='out'` row that names a member, and the `transaction_named_row_shape` trigger accepts only a dues payment or a named contribution as the target. Its test, `o.member_id = NEW.member_id`, is NULL for an unnamed row, so a reversal of an unnamed row would be refused even if the CHECK let it through (checked on a scratch copy of the schema: the insert fails today). ADR-029 refused the general case on purpose ("wider than PRD section 4 asks Uruni to be"); the maintainer has since ruled it in scope on its own merits. What this ADR changes is who may be a target, which way a reversal goes, which line of the month it lands on, and which day it is dated. How a reversal is stored does not change in kind: one linked `adjustment` row, the full amount, once. Drafted from #629 and read against #618, #627, #628, #630, #631, #632 and #633; no code exists.

## Decided before the grill

Recorded as decided (maintainer, 2026-10-19); not reopened here.

- **In scope, superseding ADR-029's refusal** of a generic reverse primitive (answered below).
- **Targets.** Of the kinds not reversible today only `normal` is added, so the targets are `dues` and `normal`. `reimbursement` payouts keep today's path (a hand-made adjusting entry; correcting a claim is Talangan's own *Perbaiki*, before it is settled). Reopening a settled claim is a separate future decision, not in this epic. `opening`, `transfer` legs and `adjustment` rows stay out. (One subset of `normal` is kept out by the same reasoning, a row a *cek kas* line names; the grill found it and the maintainer accepted it at approval, see the status argument at the foot of the Decision.)
- **The reversal copies the effective purpose** (ADR-033), not the stored one. Copying the stored purpose of a purpose-corrected row would leave the corrected-to pos debited and credit the wrong one.
- **Kept from ADR-029, ADR-034 and ADR-031:** full reversal only (no partial amount, no "reassign" - a wrong amount is reverse, then post again); a row is reversed at most once (the partial unique index); the composite FK; the ledger copies the fields and the caller types none of them; a closed envelope refuses the posting and is reopened first.
- **Rows already posted with a hand-made, unlinked reversal stay as they are** (#618). This fixes future mistakes only.

## Decision

**A posted `dues` or `normal` row is reversed by one linked entry going the other way.** The entry is a `"transaction"` row with `kind='adjustment'` and `reverses_transaction_id` set, in the **opposite** direction to its original, with the original's amount, location and **effective purpose**, and the original's `member_id` and `dues_period` exactly as they are, NULL included. The ledger copies all of them and picks the date; the caller supplies only a note. The date is the original's own day, unless a *cek kas* has counted the original since, and then today (below). A `normal` row is not reversible when a *cek kas* line names it (below). Netting the reversal into the original's kind stays rejected for ADR-029's reason (`LatestDuesPeriodPaidByMember` has no notion of direction).

**Schema.** Two edits to `00001_schema.sql` (ADR-025), and nothing else:
- The column guard loses its direction and member requirement: `CHECK (reverses_transaction_id IS NULL OR kind = 'adjustment')`. The member/period CHECK keeps ADR-034's reversal disjunct as it is: a reversal that carries a member reverses a named `in` row, so it is `out` already, and an unnamed reversal satisfies the "no member, no period" disjunct.
- The reversal half of `transaction_named_row_shape` is rewritten (a sketch; #630 owns the text):

```sql
SELECT RAISE(ABORT, 'a reversal must reverse a dues payment or a normal transaction: the other way, same amount and location, same member and period')
WHERE NEW.reverses_transaction_id IS NOT NULL
  AND NOT EXISTS (
    SELECT 1 FROM "transaction" o
    WHERE o.id = NEW.reverses_transaction_id
      AND o.kind IN ('dues', 'normal')
      AND o.direction <> NEW.direction
      AND o.amount = NEW.amount AND o.account_id = NEW.account_id
      AND o.member_id IS NEW.member_id AND o.dues_period IS NEW.dues_period
  );
```

- **Null-safety** is `IS`, not `=`: `o.member_id IS NEW.member_id` is true for two unnamed rows and for two equal members. It is the line that was wrong for unnamed rows.
- **Direction, amount and location join the trigger.** ADR-029's standard is that the schema makes the wrong row unrepresentable, and with the CHECK no longer pinning `out`, a same-direction "reversal" would add to the balance and hide its original from every query that skips reversed rows. The purpose is the one copied field the trigger does not check: the effective purpose is a fold over correction pairs, which a trigger could only restate. The footing tests hold it. The date rule (below) is not in the trigger either: restore keeps the triggers on, and a reversal already in a live file may predate its original (the ledger never checked), so a date rule there would refuse a good backup.
- The composite FK and the reversed-once index stand (the index keeps its name, `dues_payment_reversed_once`: it is the index, and renaming it would rewrite ADR-043's draft text that quotes it). Checked on the scratch copy: accepted are an ordinary `out`, an ordinary `in`, a named contribution and a dues payment; refused are a same-direction row, another amount, another location, an `opening` or `adjustment` target (so also a reversal of a reversal), an unnamed reversal carrying a member, a named one without its member, a dues one without its period, a second reversal, and a non-adjustment claiming to reverse. That matrix becomes #630's db tests.

**The ledger method and its refusals.** `Ledger.ReverseTransaction(ctx, ReverseTransactionParams{FundID, TransactionID, Note, Now})` is `ReverseDuesPayment` renamed and widened: one `withTx`, a fund-scoped fetch, and the same fields less the date, which the ledger now picks. `Now` is the injected clock every dated write carries (zero means `time.Now()`); it is read only for "today", in the fund's zone. There is no amount, location, purpose, member or date field. The refusals are one pure function, `ledger.ReversalRefusal(ReversalFacts, today)`, which the write calls with the facts of `GetReversalTarget` and `GET /api/transactions` calls with the facts of `ListTransactionsPage`, so the wire's `reversible` and the write's answer are one rule and cannot drift. Each refusal is its own named error and wire code, never one blanket rule (ADR-033's convention); the sentinel names are proposals and the codes are the contract.

| Target or state | Error | Wire |
|---|---|---|
| no such row in this fund | `ErrReversalNotFound` | 404 `not_found` |
| `opening` | `ErrReversalOpening` | 409 `reversal_opening` |
| `reimbursement` payout | `ErrReversalReimbursement` | 409 `reversal_reimbursement` |
| `transfer` leg | `ErrReversalTransfer` | 409 `reversal_transfer` |
| an `adjustment` that reverses | `ErrReversalOfReversal` | 409 `reversal_of_reversal` |
| any other `adjustment` (the `adjusted` plug a *cek kas* line names, or a standalone one) | `ErrReversalAdjustment` | 409 `reversal_adjustment` |
| a `normal` row a *cek kas* line names (an `entry_added` fix) | `ErrReversalReconciliationFix` | 409 `reversal_reconciliation_fix` |
| already reversed | `ErrAlreadyReversed` | 409 `transaction_already_reversed` (replaces `dues_payment_already_reversed`) |
| effective purpose is a closed envelope | `ErrIncidentalClosed` (ADR-031) | 409 `incidental_closed` |
| a *cek kas* has counted the original and it is dated after today, so the date the reversal would take precedes it (the assertion below; only a row from before #632's ceiling can be there) | `ErrReversalBeforeOriginal` | 409 `reversal_before_original` |

The *cek kas* refusal is one `EXISTS` on `reconciliation_line.adjustment_transaction_id` in the fund (why, in the status argument below). The closed-envelope check reads the **effective** purpose, since that is where the reversal posts: a row corrected out of a closed envelope is reversible, and one corrected into a closed envelope needs it reopened first. A reversal inherits its location from the original even if the location has since been retired, as `ReverseDuesPayment` and `PostPurposeCorrection` do today (the `ErrAccountInactive` note: refusing would make a mistake on a retired location uncorrectable). That is unchanged.

**The date is the ledger's, and the note is hers.**
- *Date.* There is no date input. The request carries a note only, and the ledger picks the date from one fact: whether a *cek kas* has counted the original since it was posted. **If none has, the reversal takes the original's `occurred_on`.** It nets in the same month on the same walk line, so no total goes negative; she types no date, so there is no ceiling or zone question ([ADR-041](./041-the-funds-time-zone.md), [#632](https://github.com/kerti/uruni/issues/632)); and ADR-033's correction pair already inherits the original's date. The one past figure that moves, the original's month ceasing to show the mistake, is what ADR-037 already accepts for any late row. **If a count has covered it, the reversal takes today** in the fund's zone, from the ledger's injected clock. A count freezes the balance of every location it counted, and a reversal dated back before it would change a past location balance under a frozen snapshot: a count on 15 April froze *cocok* at 1,000, and the original reversed on its March date would make the walk say March ended at 1,050 (checked on a scratch copy: 1,000 becomes 1,050). Nothing in the app moves a past location balance under a count by itself (a backdated posting is the treasurer's own act, ADR-024); this would be the first. Dated today, the change shows in the next *cek kas*, openly. Rejected: always the original's date (it rewrites history under a frozen count), and the previous editable date, defaulting to today (it asks a question the server can answer).
- *Counted, exactly.* A count's `reconciliation.through_transaction_id` is the fund's highest transaction id when it was taken (`MaxTransactionIDByFund`, read before the count's own fixes are posted, so those fixes sit above their own cutoff), and NULL for a count of an empty ledger. It is one per snapshot and per fund, not per location: the lines are per location, but a count must include every active location. So the original is covered when some `reconciliation` of its fund has `through_transaction_id >= original.id`, which is `original.id <= MAX(through_transaction_id)` since cutoffs never fall (ids only grow and no row is deleted); no count, or only NULL cutoffs, covers nothing. A location retired before a count was not asked about, but its older rows read as covered too, which errs towards the dated-today branch, the safe one. The test compares two ids, the cutoff's own meaning (ADR-024; ADR-012: "a ledger cutoff whose meaning is id ordering"), the one place the repo relies on id order; a count's `performed_at` is never compared with a date. Checked on a scratch copy: the row at the cutoff is covered, the next id is not, and a NULL-cutoff count covers nothing.
- *The floor and the ceiling.* The date is the original's own or today, and neither precedes the original, so #632's floor and "a reversal does not come before what it reverses" hold with no check. The ledger keeps the second as an assertion for the one case that can trip it: a covered original dated after today (only a row from before #632's ceiling can be; the picker has stopped at today since 2026-10-01), where today would precede it. It refuses that (`reversal_before_original`) rather than post a reversal ahead of what it reverses, and the hand-made opposite entry is the way out, as for every kind this ADR leaves out. An original no count has covered is reversed on its own date even if that is ahead. The assertion is not in the trigger (above). #632's ceiling does not apply: the caller supplies no date, and a date the ledger picks or inherits is not ceiling-checked (ADR-041).
- *Note.* Her own, optional ("Alasan"), never copied from the original. The original's note says what the original was; copying it would print the same words twice in Riwayat, the report and the PDF, and would put app-chosen text in a field that is hers (`docs/Decisions.md` on #257: a row the app posts explains itself by a label derived at display time, never by stored text; ADR-031: "a note nobody wrote"). The pair is linked, so a reader sees the original's note on the original.

**The route is `POST /api/transactions/{id}/reversal`** with `{note}` (the decoder ignores unknown fields, so a stray `occurred_on` is dropped, never honoured), answering 201 with the posted row as `transactionResponse`, in the nested-verb idiom of `/api/transactions/{id}/purpose-correction` and `/api/incidentals/{purposeID}/close`: the route names the row, the verb the action. **It replaces `POST /api/dues-payments/{id}/reversal`**, which is removed in the same PR as its one client function (`reverseDuesPayment` in `web/src/lib/dues.ts` changes its URL), with no alias. The old route's own handler comment says "there is no dues-payment entity, only transaction rows"; two addresses for one verb would be a second thing to keep in step for no safety gain, since a stale PWA bundle calling the removed route gets a refusal, never a wrong write, and a reload fixes it. The route is once-only by the schema, so it carries no idempotency key (below).

**A purpose correction and a reversal of the same row.** The two orders differ, and the line falls where the arithmetic does:
- *Correct, then reverse: allowed.* The reversal copies the effective purpose, so the money comes back out of the pos it is now in (decided above). The correction's own pair stays; it nets to zero by itself.
- *Reverse, then correct: refused*, with a new named error and code (`ErrPurposeCorrectionAlreadyReversed`, 409 `purpose_correction_already_reversed`; not `..._reversed`, which is one letter from the reversal row's own `..._reversal` below, and a slip between the two would refuse the wrong thing). It is reachable from a stale page (the row was reversed in another tab, or after the page loaded), so it gets copy. The reversal already took the row's money out of its effective pos; moving the original afterwards would put the pair's legs on a pos that no longer holds it and leave the reversal netting against the wrong one (worked: an `out` of 100 on A, reversed `in` on A, then corrected A to B, leaves A at +100 and B at -100). Allowing it would need a second pair for the reversal, which is not eligible. The way out is not needed: a reversed row is dead, and the right entry is posted anew.
- *The reversal row itself* is never eligible for a purpose correction. ADR-033 already refuses an `adjustment` with `reverses_transaction_id` set; that refusal (`ErrPurposeCorrectionDuesReversal`, `purpose_correction_dues_reversal`, copy "Pos pembatalan iuran...") now covers every reversal and is renamed `ErrPurposeCorrectionReversal`, `purpose_correction_reversal`, copy "Pos pembatalan tidak bisa diperbaiki." (proposed). ADR-033's Eligible clause is narrowed by both (named at the foot).
- The client hides the correction control on a reversed row (`canCorrectPurpose` reads the wire flag below).

**Where a reversal lands on the walk: on its original's own line, in the month it is dated.** The line follows the reversal's own direction, so the classifier needs no join to the original:

| Row | Line |
|---|---|
| `adjustment` with `reverses_transaction_id`, going out (it reverses income: an `in` row) | Total masuk, **negative** (today's rule, now the special case) |
| the same going in (it reverses spending: an `out` row) | Total keluar, **negative** |

Reversing an `out` lowers *Keluar*; reversing an `in` lowers *Masuk*. *Total keluar* is a magnitude and the walk adds it today, so `ReportWalk.add` subtracts a reversal from it, and `classifyReportRow` splits the one reversal case by direction. An uncounted original is reversed on its own day, in its own month, and nets there. Either total can therefore read below zero only after a *cek kas*: the original is covered, the reversal is dated today, and it lands in a later month than its original and outweighs what it nets against (a mistake from last month, counted since, reversed this month). That is **accepted, the same trade-off as ADR-038's negative *Masuk* (maintainer, 2026-10-05); #629's own text already extends it to *Keluar* ("either can read below zero in a month")**, narrowed here by the date rule to that one case, and it is revisited under the same condition: if treasurers find it confusing. The ledger itself stays gross (two rows). The footing holds either way, because every row still lands on exactly one line with its own signed effect (checked on a scratch ledger holding every row shape, with reversals in later months and across pos: start plus the lines equals the end, in every month, for the fund and for each pos). A reversal filters in the report as its line (*Uang keluar* for the reversal of an `out`), so the report's list, filter and totals still agree. Row signs and arrows, on the report page and PDF as in Riwayat, and Riwayat's own direction filter, keep the stored direction, the balance's view, as they do for a dues reversal today: the reversal of an `out` row reads `+` while it lowers *Total keluar*, and its label says why. Beranda's *Arus kas bulan ini* reads the walk through `reportRows` and needs no change of its own.

**The other readers of a reversal.**
- *The envelope's totals need a rewrite, because the pair can now straddle two pos.* `IncidentalActivityTotals` drops a reversed row and its reversal whole (`reverses_transaction_id IS NULL` plus the `NOT EXISTS`). That is right while both halves sit in one pos, and wrong after a purpose correction: an unnamed contribution of 100 corrected from A to B and then reversed has its original and legs counted against A, its reversal against B, and both balances are 0 while the query reads A collected -100 and B collected +100 (checked on the scratch copy). The query reads a reversal the way it already reads a correction's legs: as a minus on the bucket of the row it points at, in whichever pos it sits (the join already there to `corrects_transaction_id` takes `COALESCE(corrects_transaction_id, reverses_transaction_id)`, with its fund read from `t`, not `tr`: `tr` is NULL on a reversal row, and written the other way every reversal reads as spending). Inside one pos that is the same figure as dropping the pair, so no pair that exists today reads differently; across two it is the only reading where collected minus disbursed equals the balance; and the two drop-the-pair filters go. Checked over plain, same-pos reversed, named and cross-pos rows: A reads 500 collected and 200 disbursed, B 0 and 0, against balances of 300 and 0. Also checked on random ledgers with random `through` bounds: wherever no pair straddles two pos the old and new queries agree on every case, and the new one always satisfies collected minus disbursed equals the balance less the roll legs. `through` then bounds each half by its own date, so these totals agree with the bounded balances whatever the date rule above does (a legacy reversal dated before its original reads as its own minus on the original's bucket at an earlier bound, as the bounded balance does; today's query reads zero there). The pair could not straddle before this ADR, so this is a defect the ADR would otherwise introduce, not one that exists today. `ContributedByIncidentalMember` is unchanged: a named contribution refuses purpose correction (ADR-034), so its pair never straddles.
- *The Iuran history list leaks today.* `ListDuesPaymentsPage` selects every `adjustment` that reverses; since ADR-034 that includes a named contribution's reversal (member set, no period), and an unnamed reversal is kept out only by its INNER JOIN on `member`. #630 narrows it to dues reversals (`dues_period IS NOT NULL`).
- *Riwayat, the detail dialog, the report and the PDF read the reversed flag from #627's view,* and `GET /api/transactions` rows carry it as a boolean, with a second one, `reversible` (the name is the slice's), true exactly when the ledger would accept the row, and a third, `counted`, true when a *cek kas* has counted the row, which picks the date line (below). Today the client derives "reversed" from the rows on the loaded page and falls back on the 409; with the flags on the wire, a row reversed on a page not yet fetched no longer offers the control or the correction, and the client keeps no copy of the ledger's eligibility rule.

**Entry point: the row's detail in Riwayat, handing off to the inline form that exists.** In Riwayat > Transaksi the entry detail (`?entry=<id>`, opened by tapping the card) gains **Batalkan transaksi** on a row the ledger would accept. Whether it shows is the server's answer, `reversible` on the page row, not a rule the client re-derives from kind, direction and whichever reversals it has loaded: ADR-032 does not let the client work out what is owed either, and the *cek kas* case above is a fact the client cannot see at all. The button posts nothing itself. It closes the dialog and opens, under that row, the inline form named contributions already use (reason and confirm, with a line stating the date the reversal will take; `ContributionReversalControl` in `TransactionList.tsx`, which loses its date field and with it its private `todayISODate`), so ADR-032's rule stands: a dialog posts nothing that moves money, and the read-only dialog of #359 still edits nothing, it only gains a hand-off. The date line is the copy below, chosen by `counted` on the page row (beside `reversed` and `reversible`; the name is the slice's), because the client cannot see the counts; the ledger decides at post time, so a count that lands while the form is open wins, and the success line states no date. `reversible` is the ledger's whole answer, the assertion above included. Beranda's recent five stay inert (the handler is passed only by Riwayat, as `onCorrectPurpose` is). The per-row *Batalkan sumbangan* button moves into the same door, so one list has one way in; the cost is one tap more for a sumbangan (the card, then the button) and a rewritten journey (`amplop.spec.ts > cancel a sumbangan from Riwayat`). The Iuran screens keep their own *Batalkan*, calling the new route.

**How the pair reads.** The reversal row states what it reverses, and the original carries the *Sudah dibatalkan* mark, in Riwayat, the detail dialog, the report page and the PDF. A dues reversal's treatment is the model for the label (*Pembatalan · ...*); its badge exists today only in the Iuran tab, so Transaksi and the report gain it. In the common case, no count since, the reversal sits on the original's day, beside it in date order. The label of a reversal that names no member states the original's date (the same day then, a different one after a count), which the Riwayat page row and the report row do not carry today (the Iuran list does, as `reverses_occurred_on`): both selects join it. A reversal of a purpose-corrected row sits in the corrected-to pos while the original keeps its stored tag and its *Tags* marker; ADR-033's rule that the list shows stored tags stands, and the pair's tags read differently there by design.

**Member: copied.** A reversal of a named row carries the original's `member_id` (and a dues reversal its `dues_period`); a reversal of an unnamed row carries neither. The trigger demands it, the member filter and search then find the reversal beside the contribution, and the label can name the member. It is one rule in the ledger and one in the trigger, with no case for "named or not".

**Answering ADR-029's refusal of a generic primitive.** ADR-029 gave two reasons against it: it is "wider than PRD section 4 asks Uruni to be", and any kind with a status derived from it needs that status to follow the reversal (the whole argument against netting). Both are answered.
- *Scope.* PRD section 4's non-goals are reminders, member logins, payment gateways and bank sync, accounting journals and dashboards, and anything on the RT-OS list. Reversing a wrongly recorded entry is none of them. It is PRD section 5's "the numbers always add up", reached by rule 3's "corrections are new adjusting entries" for a case where today's only path is a hand-made pair no screen explains and no report can link.
- *Status.* This is not "reverse any row". A row is reversible exactly when every status derived from it already reads a reversal or has none. Dues (`DuesPaidByPeriod`, `LatestDuesPeriodPaidByMember` skip a reversed row) and a named contribution (participation and the envelope's totals already read its reversal) qualify, and so does a `normal` row, with one exception the grill found: a `normal` row a *cek kas* line names. An `entry_added` fix is `kind='normal'`, `reconciliation_line.adjustment_transaction_id` points at it, and the line's open remainder (the difference less the signed fix) is derived through it in four queries (`ListReconciliationLines`, `ListOpenReconciliationLinesByFund`, `ListReconciliationsPage`, `ReportOpenReconciliationLines`), none of which reads a reversal. Reverse such a fix and the line goes on saying the gap is explained by an entry the ledger says never happened (checked on a scratch copy: a count of 950 against 1,000 recorded, closed by a fix `out` 50; after the reversal the remainder is still 0 and the recorded balance is 1,000 again). So the row is refused with its own code, as the `adjusted` plug is, and the hand-made opposite entry plus a new count is the way, as for every kind this ADR leaves out. The other four kinds each carry a status a reversal would leave lying, and each is refused with its own code: a payout is a claim's settled state (`reimbursement_settled_once`), an opening is the *Saldo awal* line and `opening_balance_once_per_account`, an adjustment may be the entry a *cek kas* line names (`reconciliation_line.adjustment_transaction_id`), and a transfer leg is half of a value-neutral pair (a mirror transfer is value-neutral too and never enters a month's *Masuk* or *Keluar*, so refusing the leg costs none of #618's complaint). The boundary is a rule with a reason per side, not a width. ADR-029's other narrower shape, a `dues_void` kind, stays dropped.

## What was weighed and refused

- **Reverse an `out` as positive *Masuk*** (ADR-038's rule unchanged). It is #618's own complaint: the month inflated on both sides.
- **A *Pembatalan* line in the walk**, or excluding both halves from the totals. A new line for a rare event, and excluding a pair across two months breaks the footing; the reversal must land somewhere in its own month, and its original's line is the one where the net is what the neighbour expects.
- **Clamping a total at zero.** Breaks the footing the tests assert.
- **Copying the original's note, or prefixing it with app text.** Above.
- **Always taking the original's date.** It rewrites history under a frozen count: a count on 15 April froze *cocok* at 1,000, and the reversal would make March end at 1,050. Past the first count the date is today.
- **The previous editable date, defaulting to today.** It asks a question the server can answer, on a phone, and brings #632's ceiling and ADR-041's zone onto a field that needs neither.
- **Refusing to reverse a purpose-corrected row.** The simplest rule, and it contradicts the decided effective-purpose copy.
- **Allowing a correction of a reversed row** by re-pointing the reversal. A second pair for a row that is not eligible, and nothing a treasurer would recognise.
- **Keeping `/api/dues-payments/{id}/reversal`** as the one route, as a second route with its own narrower targets, or as an alias for a release. It names an entity that does not exist, puts two target sets on one ledger method, or leaves a standing second address.
- **A per-row *Batalkan* button on every `normal` row.** ADR-033's density argument: +44px on the app's densest unbounded list, for an action used rarely.
- **The form inside the detail dialog.** It would be a money-moving, two-field posting in a dialog, which ADR-032's rule forbids and would need its own superseding clause.
- **A new screen or route for reversals, a long-press, swipe or overflow menu, or an entry from Catat.** A new screen is PRD section 4 territory; a menu for one item is ADR-033's "extra tap and a hidden label"; Catat is where a new entry is typed, and a reversal starts from the row it reverses.
- **Putting the `reimbursement`, `adjustment`, `opening` or `transfer` kinds in scope.** Above; the first is a later decision.
- **The trigger checking the purpose, or the date rule.** The purpose would restate ADR-033's fold in SQL; the date rule would make restore, which keeps the triggers on, refuse a live backup holding a reversal dated before its original.
- **Teaching the open-gap queries to read a reversed fix,** so an `entry_added` row could be reversed too. It holds in principle (a reversed fix fixes nothing, so the whole difference would reopen), but it is one expression in four queries of the reconciliation derivation, with their tests, in an epic that is already one schema dance, and the refusal never lies. Deferred by the maintainer until the refusal is hit in use.

## PRD section 4 check

No new screen: the action lives in the entry detail Riwayat already has and opens the inline form the app already uses. No setting, no dependency, no new entity or table (the wire gains four read-only facts: whether a row is reversed, whether it may be, whether a *cek kas* has counted it, and the original's date for a label). The one concept is the word "reversal" for what the schema already stores, and the treasurer meets none: her verb is *batalkan* and her nouns are *pembatalan* and *sudah dibatalkan*, all in the app today. Nothing in the non-goals is approached; the accounting-journal direction is the one avoided, by keeping her word (she says *batalkan* to a transaction, she does not post a reversing entry) and by refusing the kinds that would need journal-style handling. The PRD itself says nothing about reversing anything but a dues payment and a named contribution, which is a gap rather than a conflict (consequences).

## Terms

**Reversal** (pembatalan; the treasurer's verb is *batalkan*; the column is `reverses_transaction_id`) is this ADR's word for the row that reverses another; `CONTEXT.md` has no entry for it today. The **original** is the row it points at, and **reversed** is the flag #627 defines. A reversal is dated the original's day, or today once a *cek kas* has counted the original. **Adjusting entry** is `CONTEXT.md`'s and rule 3's word for any new entry that fixes an old one, and it is the genus: a reversal is one, a purpose correction (a transfer pair) and a *cek kas* plug are others; `kind='adjustment'` is only the column value a reversal and a plug share, not the genus. **Correction**, unqualified, means ADR-033's purpose correction (*perbaikan pos*, re-tags and moves no balance) and ADR-040's rate and tier corrections, except where rule 3 is quoted: a reversal is never called a correction, and a wrong amount is neither, it is a reversal and a new posting. "Cancel", "void" and "undo" are kept out of code and prose, as "reclassify" is in ADR-033; the one English verb for what a reversal does is *reverse*. "Ordinary transaction" in the issue titles means `kind='normal'`, what Catat posts. **No new Indonesian word is decided here.**

## Consequences

- **Backup and restore: a rows-only change.** No column or table is added, `reverses_transaction_id` is already exported, and `format_version` stays where #624 puts it. `transaction_named_row_shape` is a `BEFORE INSERT` trigger and is not among `immutableTriggers`, so it stays on during restore and judges every reversal row; restore already inserts a reversal after the row it names. The v2 golden gains an ordinary reversal. The purpose-corrected-then-reversed row (its reversal in another pos) and a reversal dated before its original are `internal/backup/restore_reversal_test.go`'s round trip instead, because two existing tests pin the golden's transfer count at three and a correction adds a transfer; a file whose reversal goes the same way as its original is refused whole by the trigger, in the same file. Format 2 still imports and exports; `TestRebuildTakesThePreviousReleasesBackup` stays green. The trigger is stricter than before (direction, amount, location), so the maintainer's `make upgrade-rehearsal` on both live backups ([#636](https://github.com/kerti/uruni/issues/636)) is the proof that no live reversal fails it; a dues and a contribution reversal restored from a v1 file are in the tests, and so is a restore of one whose reversal is dated before its original, which the trigger must not refuse.
- **Tests.** The walk's footing tests (ADR-038), for the whole fund and each pos: a reversed `out` and a reversed `in` (each on the original's day, nothing negative), a counted original reversed today in a later month (a negative *Keluar* and a negative *Masuk*), a purpose-corrected original reversed after a count (both pos walks, original and reversal in different months), and a dues and a contribution reversal. The envelope-totals equation over the cross-pos pair. The trigger matrix above. One test per refusal code (the *cek kas* one for an `entry_added` fix as well as the `adjusted` plug), correction-after-reversal, and the closed-envelope check on the effective purpose. Fund, account and pos balances return to their pre-original values for a reversed `out`, a reversed `in` and a corrected-then-reversed row. The date rule: an uncounted original, a dues payment included, is reversed on its own day; a counted one today, from an injected clock; at the boundary the row at the cutoff is counted and the next id is not; no count and a NULL-cutoff count count nothing; and the assertion for a counted original dated after today. A negative *Keluar* formatted on Beranda, the report page and the PDF.
- **Existing tests that change meaning.** #630's "their existing tests are untouched" cannot hold for three: `TestReverseDuesPaymentRefusesANonDuesTransaction` and `TestReverseDuesPaymentRefusesAnUnnamedContribution` (ledger) and `TestPostDuesPaymentReversalNonDuesTransactionIs400` (http) assert that an ordinary row is refused. They become tests that it succeeds, with refusal tests retargeted at the kinds that stay out. The rest (dues status, paid-in-advance, participation, envelope totals for dues and contributions) keep their expectations, which is also what catches a wrong join in the envelope rewrite. Beyond those three, the reversal's date leaves the API: about sixty call sites in sixteen test files pass one (the dues, contribution, walk, month-end, balances and dues-status tests among them) and lose the argument. The ones that need a reversal in a later month than its original build it as production does, a count between the two and the injected clock, or post the row directly; `TestReverseDuesPaymentRejectsInvalidOccurredOn` goes with the input it tested.
- **Code.** The ledger method and route above; `classifyReportRow` and `ReportWalk.add`; the `IncidentalActivityTotals` rewrite (and its comment "the corrected row is never a reversal", which stops being true); `ListDuesPaymentsPage`'s narrowing; the Riwayat and report selects take the reversed flag from #627's view, a server-computed `reversible`, and the original's date for the unnamed label (`reverses_occurred_on`); the `ErrPurposeCorrection...` renames (`ErrPurposeCorrectionReversal`, `ErrPurposeCorrectionAlreadyReversed`); the client's `canCorrectPurpose`, `canReverseContribution` (replaced by the flag), `ContributionReversalControl` and the `lib/dues.ts` URL; both reversal forms (`MemberPayments.tsx` and `ContributionReversalControl`) lose their date field and gain the date line, `ReverseDuesPaymentParams.OccurredOn` and the request's `occurred_on` go, the ledger gains the cutoff read and the injected clock, and `counted` joins the page rows; and the names and comments that say dues or contribution where they now mean any row (`GetDuesPaymentReversal`, the `reverseDuesPayment` handler and client function, `transactionResponse.ReversesTransactionID`, the schema column's comment, `MonthInOut`'s "Out is a magnitude").
- **A reversal moves the balance, unlike a purpose correction.** A *cek kas* that already plugged the difference the wrong row caused will read *selisih* by the plug after the reversal, and she counts again; the plug is an `adjustment`, and a fix she added during the count is a row the count names, so neither is reversible. A reversal posted after a count has a higher id, so it stays outside that snapshot's arithmetic and inside today's balance (ADR-024, ADR-031), and past a count it is dated today, so no past location balance moves under a frozen snapshot.
- **[#633](https://github.com/kerti/uruni/issues/633) and [#635](https://github.com/kerti/uruni/issues/635).** Reversals are once-only routes and keep their 409; the partial unique index is the idempotency, so a retried reversal whose first answer was lost gets `transaction_already_reversed`, and the UI may treat that as success, as the contribution control's re-read already does. A retry with another note lands on the first attempt's, which is fine. The route's new name is the one #633's list means by "reversals".
- **[#632](https://github.com/kerti/uruni/issues/632).** The caller supplies no date, so the ceiling does not apply to a reversal (a date the ledger picks or inherits is not ceiling-checked, ADR-041), the floor is met by construction, and the "not before the original" assertion above is the only date check. Today comes from the injected clock in the fund's zone that #632's ceilings and ADR-041 already add to the write paths; the forms need no zone of their own, since they show a date and never default one.
- **Dues reversals change too, a shipped path** (the ledger and route in #630, the Iuran form in #631). Today `ReverseDuesPayment` takes a caller-supplied `occurred_on` (the Iuran form defaults it to today and lets her pick back two years) and refuses the request without one. After this ADR the request is a note, the date is the payment's own day unless a *cek kas* has counted the payment since (then today), and the form loses its date field and gains the date line. Unchanged: dues status (`DuesPaidByPeriod` and `LatestDuesPeriodPaidByMember` read whether a reversal exists, never its date), the reversal's fields, the once-only index and the closed-envelope check. Changed: an uncounted dues reversal now nets in the payment's month instead of the month she reverses it, so the negative *Masuk* ADR-038 accepted for dues reversals arises only after a count, and the Iuran history list shows the reversal beside its payment instead of at the top.
- **Docs, by their owners.** `CONTEXT.md` gains **Reversal** under Movements, from the Terms above (#636's close-out lists it). `PRD.md` section 7.2 gains a line that a transaction recorded in error is reversed by a new entry, never edited away, on the original's day unless a *cek kas* has counted it since (then today) (section 7.3's dues line and section 11's "reverse, then post again" stay), mirrored in `PRD-ID.md`; both are the maintainer's to word, and #636's list of docs does not name them yet. `docs/Decisions.md` ("reversal scope", #636) is the orchestrator's. `web/e2e/COVERAGE.md` gets the journey's rows (ADR-039), with both date lines, and the sumbangan journey's row is rewritten for the new door; neither existing reversal journey drives a date field (both accept the default), so none loses coverage when the field goes. ADR-029, ADR-033, ADR-034 and ADR-038 are marked partly superseded, in the strikethrough-and-pointer shape ADR-031 used, **in #630's PR** with the code that makes it true and not now, since the old clauses stay true until then; their index rows stay `implemented`. The ADR index gains this ADR's row, `draft` (the orchestrator's; it has none yet). ADR-032 and ADR-031 are read, not marked (below). This ADR's own tag comes off at the epic's close, not in the first slice.
- **Accepted: a total can read below zero,** *Keluar* as well as *Masuk*, but only for a counted original reversed today in a later month; and Beranda can show it.
- **Accepted: an uncounted reversal moves the original's month,** so a month a neighbour has read can change when its mistake is reversed. ADR-037 already accepts a past month moving when a late row lands; the count rule is what stops a reversal from rewriting the past under a frozen count.
- **Accepted: a reversal posts to a retired location** if the original sat on one, leaving a retired location non-zero. It is the existing trade (rule 3 over tidiness), recorded because reversals become common.
- **Accepted: a reversed row cannot be re-tagged,** and the pair of a corrected-then-reversed row shows two different tags in the list.
- **Accepted: a fix she added during a *cek kas* cannot be reversed.** She posts the opposite entry by hand and counts again, which is today's path.
- **Accepted: a counted row dated after today cannot be reversed until its date** (today would precede it), and only a row from before #632 can be there.

## Settled at approval (2026-10-20)

- **The date is the ledger's, not hers.** The reversal takes the original's `occurred_on`, unless a *cek kas* has counted the original since, and then today in the fund's zone (above). There is no date input on the Transaksi form or the Iuran form, the request is a note, and the confirm step states the date. Rejected: always the original's date (it rewrites history under a frozen count), and the editable date.
- **A `normal` row a *cek kas* line names is refused** (`reversal_reconciliation_fix`). Teaching the four open-gap queries to read a reversed fix is deferred until the refusal is hit in use.
- **`POST /api/dues-payments/{id}/reversal` is removed with no alias,** in #630, with its one client function.
- **One door:** *Batalkan sumbangan* moves into the entry detail's *Batalkan transaksi* (one tap more for a sumbangan; one e2e journey rewritten).
- **The button shows on dues rows in Transaksi too,** on the server's `reversible`, so the client keeps no copy of the rule.
- **Negative totals are accepted,** *Keluar* as well as *Masuk*, narrowed by the date rule to a counted original reversed today in a later month; Beranda can show one.
- **The stricter trigger** (opposite direction, same amount, same location) is accepted. ADR-012 keeps the triggers on during restore for exactly this, and the rehearsal on both live backups ([#636](https://github.com/kerti/uruni/issues/636)) is the proof for live data.
- **One success line on Transaksi,** "Transaksi dibatalkan."; the Iuran screens keep theirs.
- **The grill's additions are accepted:** the ADR-033 clause at the foot (ADR-032 and ADR-031 read, not marked); `reversed`, `reversible`, `counted` and the original's date on the page rows; the envelope rewrite's join reading its fund from `t`; the adjusting-entry genus in Terms; `purpose_correction_reversal` and `purpose_correction_already_reversed` as the two names; the tests and code lists.
- **Copy:** the date line, the one success line and the button are approved (above); the rest of the proposal goes to the maintainer in #631, after the sweep.
