# ADR-048 - A dues payment is one row

**Status:** implemented · M11 ([#758](https://github.com/kerti/uruni/issues/758)) · grilled with the maintainer 2026-11-04 · amends [ADR-042](./042-reversing-any-ordinary-transaction.md) (a dues payment is reversed whole) · [ADR index](./README.md)

**Context.** A dues payment covering several months posts one `kind='dues'` row per month ([ADR-027](./027-ledger-domain-boundary.md)), because status, arrears, the 12-month grid and the report read dues month by month. Riwayat, Beranda and the report therefore showed N rows for what the treasurer did once. Nothing stored tied a payment's rows together: they share member, location, date, pos and note, and `created_at` only by luck, so two separate same-day payments could not be told from one multi-month payment. A display-only heuristic would sometimes be wrong.

**Decision.**

**A `dues_payment` record binds a payment's rows.** `dues_payment(id, fund_id, created_at)` holds nothing else; every `kind='dues'` row carries `dues_payment_id` (a composite FK, as `transfer_id` does), required on dues rows and refused on every other kind. `PostDuesPayments` inserts the header, then the rows, in the one database transaction it already opens; nothing is ever updated. A single-month payment has a header too, so every reader has one path.

The rule behind it: **when one act writes several rows and nothing already stored ties them, they are bound by a record named for that act.** A second binder beside `transfer`, not a kind of it, and not a general grouping table: `transfer`'s columns and CHECKs belong to a pair ([ADR-033](./033-correcting-a-posted-purpose.md)), and one table for every group of rows is the accounting journal PRD section 4 rules out. **Revisit trigger:** if a second non-transfer multi-row act is filed before v1.0.0 (paying back several talangan in one handover is the likely one), merge the binders into one general table then, while the schema is still edited in place ([ADR-025](./025-one-migration-file-until-1.0.md)); after the freeze a merge is a real migration.

**A dues payment is reversed whole.** *Batalkan* on a payment writes one reversal row per month still standing, in one transaction; each row keeps ADR-042's checks, and all share one date by its cek kas rule, since the months share a date and a location. A wrong month is fixed by reversing the payment and recording it again. A month picker was rejected: it makes a half-reversed payment the normal state, adds a control to a confirm ([ADR-046](./046-confirms-on-one-record-are-dialogs.md)), and needs a stored reversal record to keep two same-day reversals apart. Partly reversed payments exist only in data from before this ADR.

**Reversal rows need no record of their own.** Each names the row it undoes, which names its payment, so a reversal group is derived: (the reversed rows' payment, the reversal's date). A `dues_reversal` table was rejected: it would store that link a second time, with an invariant (its payment equals every reversed row's payment) only a trigger could hold. Accepted cost: a pre-ADR single-month reversal and a later reversal of the rest, on the same day, show as one row - which is true, and only old data can produce it.

**One row per group, everywhere a list shows rows.** Riwayat, Beranda, the public report and its PDF show a payment as `Iuran Mei-Jun, Okt 2026 - <member>` with the sum of its rows, its months listed in the detail; a reversal group likewise as `Dibatalkan: Iuran ...`. Grouping happens in the query **before** pagination, as Riwayat's transfer collapse already does, so a page never splits a group. The row keeps the amount recorded on the day; reversals stay their own rows (a reversal dated today by the cek kas rule lands in another month). States: nothing reversed; *Sebagian dibatalkan* (old data only; *batalkan* takes the rest); *Sudah dibatalkan*. Balances, dues status, the 12-month grid and the report's dues section read rows as before: a group is identical in every filtered or summed dimension but period and amount, so no filter changes meaning.

**Receipts stay on rows.** `receipt` keeps its two parents (transaction, claim). A group shows every photo on any of its rows; a new photo added from a group goes on its **anchor row**, the row of its earliest month - fixed, and never chosen by id order. The same rule serves payment groups and reversal groups, and any later group, with or without a record, and a regrouping never moves evidence. Rejected: photos on the `dues_payment` record (asymmetric with reversal groups, which have none, and a third receipt parent that grows with every binder). Accepted cost: in the backup JSON a payment's slip sits on its first month.

**Existing data, backup format 3.** The export gains `dues_payments` and `transactions[].dues_payment_id`; `FormatVersion` becomes 3, and the 2-to-3 step in the importer binds a format 2 file's dues rows: rows of one fund with the same member, location, date and note, each at most one second after the previous by `created_at`, form one payment; any other row is a payment of its own. A second boundary inside one payment's insert loop is caught; two separate payments inside two seconds are not something a person can record. The live instances upgrade by backup, fresh image and restore, so the step runs there; their borderline cases (same member, location, date and note, 2 to 120 seconds apart) are reviewed by hand with the maintainer before the restore.

**Consequences.**

- One new table and one column on `transaction`; the backup format moves to 3 with an upgrade step.
- The list query, the report query and the API's list item learn groups; the reversal endpoint learns to reverse a payment; the receipt viewer reads a group's photos.
- ADR-042's "once only" now reads per row within a payment: a payment is reversed by one act, never month by month.
