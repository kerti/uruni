# ADR-041 — The fund's time zone: WIB, WITA or WIT

**Status:** Accepted · implemented in the Production milestone (epic [#523](https://github.com/kerti/uruni/issues/523): [#639](https://github.com/kerti/uruni/issues/639) the zone, [#632](https://github.com/kerti/uruni/issues/632) the date-window ceilings that read it). It supersedes the Asia/Jakarta clauses of [ADR-035](./035-public-report.md), [ADR-037](./037-report-shows-the-month-as-it-ended.md) and [ADR-038](./038-the-month-as-a-balance-walk.md), named below · [ADR index](./README.md)

**Context.** Every "today" and every month in Uruni is reckoned in one hard-coded zone. `internal/tz` loads Asia/Jakarta once, and the code reads it in eight places directly (the ledger's dues period and report, the members route, the backup download's name, the e2e seed) and nine more through `internal/backup`'s private alias (dump and pre-upgrade copy names, the "has today got its dump" check, the restore preview). The reason is sound and stays: a container runs UTC, so an instant read in the host's zone puts a treasurer's evening in the wrong day, and on a month's last evening in the wrong month ([#324](https://github.com/kerti/uruni/issues/324), [#379](https://github.com/kerti/uruni/issues/379)). What the code comment gives for fixing the zone, "nothing in the PRD, CONTEXT.md or an ADR names a per-fund time zone, and there is no multi-region story yet", was true when it was written.

The web app reads the device's zone instead: `todayISODate` and `currentPeriod` in `web/src/lib/dates.ts`, and twelve private copies of the same two helpers in screens and components (`RecordTransaction`, `Reconcile`, `Incidentals`, `TransactionList`, `Dues/RecordPayment`, `Dues/MemberPayments`, `Dues/Status`, `Reimbursements/helpers`, `Setup`, `DuesTier`, `Settings/DuesTiers`). A phone in WIB agrees with the server; a phone in WITA (one hour ahead) or WIT (two hours ahead) does not: between local midnight and 01:00 or 02:00 the form defaults to a date the server's day has not reached. Nothing refuses it today, because the server accepts any real date. [#632](https://github.com/kerti/uruni/issues/632)'s ceilings will, and then the form's own default date would be refused for that hour, every night.

**Eastern Indonesia is not an edge case.** A fund in Makassar or Jayapura counts its days and its months like one in Jakarta, and the app that holds its money should turn the day over when the community does, not when Jakarta does. The maintainer's ruling (2026-10-20): Eastern Indonesia is treated as equal to Western Indonesia, not as an edge case. The zone is therefore a fact about the fund, the way its currency is, not about the server or the phone. Drafted 2026-10-20 from [#638](https://github.com/kerti/uruni/issues/638) and grilled the same day.

The ground it stands on is [ADR-024](./024-schema-conventions.md)'s two time types. A business date (`occurred_on`, `joined_on`) and a period (`dues_period`) name a calendar day or month: text, never an instant. An audit instant (`created_at`, `performed_at`) is UTC unix seconds. A time zone matters in exactly one place: where the system turns an instant into a business date or period (today, the current month, the month a *cek kas* falls in), or a month's first day back into an instant (the bound of a past month). This ADR is about that place and nothing else. Two words are kept apart throughout: the **zone** is the fund's time zone; a **clock** is only a source of the current instant (`api.now`, `ReportParams.Now`, the device's). A day or a month needs both.

## Decision

**Decided by the maintainer (2026-10-20), recorded as decided and not reopened here:**

- The zone lives on the fund and the treasurer chooses it. Rejected: **an operator env var** (`URUNI_TZ` or the container's `TZ`). The zone belongs to the community, not to the machine; the treasurer is often not the operator; the unauthenticated public report must read it from somewhere that is not a request; and a zone that is not in the database does not travel with a backup, so a restore onto another host would silently change every month boundary.
- The column is `fund.time_zone TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'Asia/Jakarta' CHECK (time_zone IN ('Asia/Jakarta', 'Asia/Makassar', 'Asia/Jayapura'))`, next to `currency` in `00001_schema.sql` ([ADR-025](./025-one-migration-file-until-1.0.md)). IANA names are stored, because Go's `time.LoadLocation` and the browser's `Intl.DateTimeFormat({timeZone})` take them as they are. WIB, WITA and WIT exist only as copy.
- Exactly the three Indonesian zones. Rejected: **every IANA zone.** A picker of four hundred names for three real answers; every extra zone is an untested case for the ceilings, the dump names and the month boundaries, and a zone with daylight saving would break the assumption this ADR leans on (below) that the offset is constant. Uruni serves Indonesian communities; a wider list is a superseding ADR, not a loosened `CHECK`.
- The default is WIB, so both live instances and every v1 backup land on today's behaviour with no backfill.
- It rides backup `format_version` 2 (bumped once for the epic in [#624](https://github.com/kerti/uruni/issues/624)): `fund` in `uruni.json` gains `time_zone`, the v1-to-v2 step in `ParseUpload` fills `Asia/Jakarta`, and restore refuses any other value through the `CHECK`. A v2 file with the field missing reads as the empty string and is refused the same way.

**One rule runs through everything below: a day or a month is an injected instant read in the fund's zone.** Never `time.Now()` plus a zone literal, never the device's zone, never a zone held in a package variable. Stored business dates and periods are never rewritten.

**Where the treasurer picks it.** Two places, both on screens that exist.
- *Setup.* One field on step 1, under the fund's name, three options, **pre-selected from the device's UTC offset** (+7, +8, +9 as WIB, WITA, WIT; any other offset falls back to WIB; read by offset, not IANA name, which aliases and a privacy-hardened browser's "UTC" would defeat), so most treasurers tap nothing. The field stays visible and editable, so a travelling treasurer is untouched; this is a starting value, not the device derivation rejected above. It leaves the browser with the rest in the one `POST /api/setup` (`time_zone` is optional there; absent means the default), and `SetUpFund` takes it as a parameter. Setup is the one place the client needs a zone before the server has a fund, so the wizard sets the client's zone from its own field (below), and again from the fund `POST /api/setup` answers with, so the roster step reads what was stored. Its opening-balance date and rate month are read in the zone she chose.
- *Pengaturan.* A second card in the fund's existing section, under the name, opens a dialog at `?edit=fund:time_zone` ([ADR-032](./032-two-level-navigation.md): every non-posting edit is a dialog), saved with `PATCH /api/fund`. The body now takes `name` and `time_zone`, each optional, at least one required; `{}` is still a 400. It is direct CRUD like the rename: no derived invariant, the `CHECK` is the validation ([ADR-027](./027-ledger-domain-boundary.md)). No confirm step: a change rewrites nothing, and setting the old zone back restores every earlier reading exactly (below).
- Rejected: a Pengaturan section or screen of its own (one field does not earn one); deriving it from the device (the treasurer may be travelling, and it is the device's clock that this ADR replaces); a free-text or "all zones" select (above).

**How the client learns it.** The fund payload carries it: `fundResponse.time_zone` on `GET /api/fund`, and on `POST /api/setup`, `PATCH /api/fund` and `POST /api/fund/report-slug`, which already answer with the whole fund. `App.tsx` already probes `GET /api/fund` once on mount and holds the result; the zone reaches `web/src/lib/dates.ts` through one setter, which holds it for the module. **The setter is called inside the functions that return a fund** (`getFund`, `postSetup`, `renameFund` and its successor for `PATCH /api/fund`, `replaceReportSlug`, all in `lib/setup.ts`), before their promise resolves, and never from an effect or during render. `AuthedGate` mounts the Shell on the very render that follows the fetch, and every `useState(todayISODate)` initialiser under it runs in that render: a zone set from an effect would let the first screen read the default, which is the 00:30 bug again, in the form she opens first. Every "today", "this month" and `DateBounds` function there formats `new Date()` with `Intl.DateTimeFormat(..., { timeZone })` (via `formatToParts`, so no locale decides the shape), and **the twelve private copies are deleted** in favour of the one. The date picker's highlighted "today" is a thirteenth reader of the device: `DateField` hands `DayPicker` the fund's today through its `today` prop, whose default is `new Date()`. Until a zone is set the helpers read the default zone, never the device's, so a test or a stray early call is deterministic, and the module exports a reset for tests. The Shell renders only after the fund resolves, so no screen asks first. A Vitest fails on `new Date()` or `Date.now()` outside `lib/dates.ts` and tests: the copies were born because the helper was not at hand, and this is what keeps a thirteenth from growing back.
- Rejected: a React context (these helpers are plain functions called from `useState` initialisers and event handlers; a context makes every call site a hook, and the copies exist because the helper was not at hand); a separate endpoint (the payload is already fetched); asking the server for "today" on each form (a round trip per form to correct a phone clock that is already right). [ADR-038](./038-the-month-as-a-balance-walk.md)'s "the client never works out a month" is about Beranda's figures, which stay the server's; a picker's bounds are a UI guard (#632) and are the client's.

**The shape of `internal/tz`.** The package stops being a fixed zone and becomes a vocabulary: the three names as constants (`Jakarta`, `Makassar`, `Jayapura`), `Default` (= `Jakarta`), `Names()` in WIB, WITA, WIT order, and `Load(name) (*time.Location, error)`, which accepts exactly those three and errors on anything else. The locations are loaded once at init from the embedded `time/tzdata` (the distroless image ships no zoneinfo; that import and its reason stay), and a failed load still panics rather than silently dating things in UTC. **No `*time.Location` variable is exported, and `tz.Jakarta` becomes a name (a string)**, so every stray `now.In(tz.Jakarta)` is a compile error. A name outside the three cannot reach a handler through the `CHECK`; if one does, the database is broken, the request answers 500 and is logged, and nothing falls back to UTC or WIB. `Default` is read only where a request omits the zone (Setup, the v1-to-v2 step) or where no fund can be read (the boot dump before setup, the two operator-file stamps below); it never dates a day of a fund that exists, and #639 greps for it to prove that.
- *Who reads the zone.* Ledger code that reckons a day reads `fund.time_zone` in the transaction it is already in (`monthlyReport` already calls `GetFund`, but after it computes `today`; the call moves up). HTTP code uses the fund `resolveFund` returned. Neither takes a zone from a request.
- *Who supplies the instant.* The existing injected clocks: `api.now`, `ReportParams.Now`, and the same on the ledger's write paths for #632's ceilings. `CurrentDuesPeriod`, a pure function, takes the fund's location with the instant; `MonthInOut` reads the fund itself.
- *Three copies of one list.* The schema's `CHECK`, `tz.Names()` and the web's list of options (one module in `web/src/lib`). One Go test reads `00001_schema.sql` and that module and fails on a differing list or default, as ADR-035's test does for the palette by reading `web/src/index.css`; no Vitest reaches out of `web/`, and none does today. The SQL never reads a clock (checked: no `'now'`, `strftime` or `localtime` in the schema or the queries), so no fourth copy exists in SQL.

**What a zone change does mid-month: it re-reads, it never rewrites.**
- *Persisted, and left alone.* Every stored business date and period: `occurred_on`, `joined_on`, `opened_on`, `closed_on`, `inactive_on`, `waived_on`, `incurred_on`, `dues_period`, `effective_from`. None is derived from the zone after the write that stamped it (`member.joined_on`'s server default, an opening balance's date), and none is touched. Every instant (`created_at`, `performed_at`, `uploaded_at`) is UTC and untouched. A dump's file name is the one stored thing that carries a wall-clock reading; it names its zone (below).
- *Persisted per month: nothing.* Checked against the schema: no table caches a month, a balance as of a month, or a report. `reconciliation.through_transaction_id` is an id cutoff, not a date. The public report is `Cache-Control: no-store` ([ADR-035](./035-public-report.md)), so nothing has to be invalidated.
- *Derived, and moves at once.* The current dues period and every arrears count, Beranda's month, the report's running month and its list of months, a past month's "latest *cek kas*" (bounded by `performed_at` before the next month's first instant), and whether a pass-through purpose had been created by then (`purpose.created_at` against the same bound, [ADR-037](./037-report-shows-the-month-as-it-ended.md)). On the instant of the change, "this month" is whatever the new zone says, exactly as it would have been had the fund always been in that zone.
- *Existing rows are never invalid.* Ceilings apply on write ([#632](https://github.com/kerti/uruni/issues/632)); restore enforces the floor only. A change to a zone further west (WIT to WIB) can leave a row, for up to two hours, dated a day ahead of the fund's new today; it stays, and the report already foots such rows ([ADR-038](./038-the-month-as-a-balance-walk.md): the running month has no upper bound).
- *No confirm, no audit row, no deferred effective date.* A change is exactly reversible, since every reading is derived: setting the old zone back restores them, and a row written in between is just a row. Rejected: a pending zone that takes effect next month (a new concept, with state, for a change that rewrites nothing); refusing the change once a fund has data (it would lock out exactly the Eastern treasurer who set up on the WIB default); a history of zones (the fund has no other history of its settings).
- *The one honest side effect.* A past month's cek kas near a month boundary can change months: a count at 23:30 WIB on 30 September reads as 01:30 WIT on 1 October, and the September report no longer shows it as that month's latest. [ADR-037](./037-report-shows-the-month-as-it-ended.md) already accepts that a past month's figures can move (a row recorded late, dated inside it); this is a second cause, bounded to a count within two hours of a boundary, and no ledger sum moves.
- *Accepted.* A second device keeps the old zone until it reloads, and an installed PWA still on the previous bundle keeps reading the device's zone until she takes the update banner; the server's refusal is the backstop, and a reload corrects it. A device clock a few minutes ahead of the server's can still offer a date the server's midnight has not reached; that is skew, not zone, and not corrected here. A restore carries the file's zone with it, as it carries the fund's name and report link: a v1 file reads as WIB, so restoring one onto an Eastern fund puts it back on WIB until she changes it again, and Pengaturan shows which.

**Daily dumps and file names.**
- *The day.* "Does today have its dump" ([ADR-012](./012-backup-and-export.md), [ADR-013](./013-scheduling-in-process.md)) compares the dump's *instant* with the current instant, both read in the fund's current zone. The hourly tick is unchanged; the scheduler reads the fund's zone on each tick (the first `ListFunds` row, [ADR-030](./030-multi-fund-scoping.md)'s rule as `resolveFund` applies it, with no other selection mechanism added), and an instance with no fund yet (the boot dump before setup) reads `tz.Default`. v1 holds one fund, so "the fund" is that row; which fund's zone an instance-wide job follows in a multi-fund world is that ADR's question, not this one's.
- *The name.* A dump's name carries wall-clock time, and a wall-clock time without its zone is ambiguous the moment the zone can change, or the file leaves the host (an off-site copy, a `backups/` folder restored onto another machine). So a dump is stamped in the fund's zone, **with a numeric-offset token naming it when the zone is not WIB**: `uruni-20261002-003000-utc8-daily-fv2-<hash>.zip`. The token is `utc8` or `utc9` (not WITA, WIT: a file name is not copy). A name with **no token** is Asia/Jakarta, whenever it was written: every existing dump keeps its meaning, and nothing a WIB instance writes changes shape. The parse reads the stamp in its own zone, so the instants stay exact: a zone change never re-dates an old file, the "newest" order never inverts, and `NeedsFormatVersionDump` and retention keep working across it. The Cadangan card's `date` is the name's own date, so the card and the saved file always agree.
- *Operator files stay as they are.* `uruni-pre-upgrade-...sqlite` is written under the instance lock before migrations, on a database whose schema may predate this column, and `uruni.db.pre-<version>-<date>-<time>` is written when the old file is not readable by the new queries. Neither can read a zone, neither is a day of the fund's, and neither is read back as one, so both keep their Asia/Jakarta (`tz.Default`) stamp. They are the only file names that do.
- *Fresh downloads.* `ZipFilename` takes the fund's date; the web names the file with the same `todayISODate`, now in the fund's zone, so the two agree for any fund, not only a WIB one.
- Rejected: stamping names in UTC (a WITA fund's daily dump is typically written in the first hour of its day, and would be named for the day before, which is the very mismatch to avoid); keeping names in WIB (the same mismatch, but only for Eastern funds, at the one time it is most visible); stamping in the fund's zone with no token and parsing in the current zone (a change re-dates every existing name by up to two hours: it can put an older dump after a newer one, which `NeedsFormatVersionDump` and retention both read, and after a change to a zone further west it makes the previous evening's dump count as today's, so the day's first changes go unbacked-up until the next day); a fixed local dump time (the hourly tick stays).

**Displayed timestamps.** `created_at` and `performed_at` (the only instants the web shows) stay UTC and are *shown* in the fund's zone. `formatUnixSeconds` and `formatUnixSecondsWithTime` take the module's zone; a time of day (the entry detail's "Dicatat") carries the zone's abbreviation, from copy and not from `Intl`'s engine-dependent `timeZoneName`: "1 Oktober 2026 pukul 13.42 WIB". The date and month formatters (`formatIsoDate`, `formatPeriod`) take a business date or period and are zone-independent and unchanged. The public report and the PDF show days and months only, never a time of day, so nothing there needs a zone name; they read the fund's zone from the fund row they already load, and the unauthenticated route learns and exposes nothing new. Restore's preview date (and `rebuild`'s printed "backup taken", the same function) and its null-`joined_on` backfill ([#471](https://github.com/kerti/uruni/issues/471)) read the zone of the document's fund (one date for the whole file; a file with no fund reads WIB) (a v1 file is WIB by the v1-to-v2 step, so nothing changes for old backups). A zone `tz.Load` refuses, unknown or the empty string a missing field reads as, is refused at inspect, since the preview cannot date the file without it; the `CHECK` stays the backstop.

**Copy (approved by the maintainer, 2026-10-20).** Swept first: `CONTEXT.md`, `PRD-ID.md` and `web/src/copy/id.ts` have no term for a time zone, and the only "waktu" in them ("berlaku menurut waktu", "titik waktu") means something else. "Zona waktu" is the ordinary word; the fund is "kas" in copy, as `Nama kas` already is.
- Field label, both places: **Zona waktu**.
- Setup hint, under the field: "Menentukan kapan hari dan bulan berganti untuk kas ini. Bisa diganti nanti di Pengaturan."
- Options, each with a plain example line:
  - "WIB - Waktu Indonesia Barat" / "mis. Jakarta, Medan, Palembang"
  - "WITA - Waktu Indonesia Tengah" / "mis. Makassar, Denpasar, Balikpapan"
  - "WIT - Waktu Indonesia Timur" / "mis. Jayapura, Ambon"
- Pengaturan: card shows the chosen option's "WITA - Waktu Indonesia Tengah"; dialog title "Ubah zona waktu"; body "Dipakai untuk tanggal hari ini, pergantian bulan, dan laporan publik. Menggantinya tidak mengubah catatan yang sudah ada."; "Simpan" and "Batal" as the rename dialog has them.
- The fund's section in Pengaturan is headed "Nama kas" and its intro speaks only of the name, so with a second card it needs a heading and an intro that cover both. Proposal: heading "Kas ini", intro "Nama kas muncul di seluruh aplikasi dan di laporan publik; zona waktunya menentukan kapan hari dan bulan berganti. Menggantinya tidak mengubah catatan apa pun." It replaces strings the tests and the e2e assert on; #639 updates them.

## Consequences

- **Every Jakarta read goes.** Direct `tz.Jakarta`: `ledger/dues_status.go` (`CurrentDuesPeriod`), `ledger/report.go` (four: `today`, the next-month bound, the cek kas date, `MonthInOut`), `http/members.go` (`joined_on`), `http/backup.go` (`ZipFilename`), `cmd/uruni/seed_e2e.go`. Through `backup`'s `jakarta` alias, which a grep for `tz.Jakarta` misses: `dumps.go` (name build and parse, the day check twice), `preupgrade.go` (two), `rebuild.go`, `restore.go` (the preview date, which `rebuild`'s printed date shares through `NewestDate`, and the backfill day). The alias and `timezone.go` go with them. `.Local()` in `cmd/uruni/migrate.go` is the operator's own clock for `migrate status`, on purpose, and stays. #624 and #625 add more reads (a tier's default start month); #639 greps again when it starts.
- **Web.** `lib/dates.ts` gains the setter and zone-aware formatters; the twelve private copies are removed; `DateField` passes the fund's today to `DayPicker`; `lib/backup.ts`'s and `lib/balances.ts`'s comments about WIB and Jakarta are corrected; `Setup.tsx` computes its dates from its own field; the fund section of Pengaturan gains the second card and the heading above.
- **Server.** `fundResponse.time_zone`; `PATCH /api/fund` takes either field; `SetUpFundParams.TimeZone`; a `GetFund`-based read in the ledger's write paths for #632's ceilings; `ListDumps` and the dump-name grammar learn the optional token.
- **Backup.** Format 2 carries `fund.time_zone` and ADR-012 is edited in the same PR as that code, per the draft rule: the format-2 field list, the dump-name grammar and "has today a dump" in the fund's zone, and the pre-upgrade paragraph's "Asia/Jakarta", which stays and gains its reason (no fund can be read then). `SELF-HOSTING.md`'s table ("Once a day (Asia/Jakarta)") and its file-name examples follow. The grammar is also matched outside `internal/backup`: by `.github/workflows/image.yml`'s boot job (a `grep -Eq` on the boot dump's name, which is written before any fund exists) and by `web/e2e/report-backup.spec.ts`, and its names are fixtures in `Backup.test.tsx` and `RestoreDialog.test.tsx`; Because a WIB name carries no token, none of these change; #639 adds `utc8` and `utc9` cases to the parser tests. `TestRebuildTakesThePreviousReleasesBackup` stays green: the v1 fixture restores as WIB.
- **#632.** Its ceilings read the fund's zone from the fund row, with `now` injected; inside `SetUpFund` the fund row does not exist yet, so the opening-balance ceiling reads the zone in the params. **Inherited dates are exempt.** A ceiling is an argument-shape check on a date a caller supplies ([ADR-027](./027-ledger-domain-boundary.md); `validateOccurredOn` is where it belongs), not a rule inside `postTransferPairTx`. A date the ledger inherits rather than receives, [ADR-033](./033-correcting-a-posted-purpose.md)'s pair taking the corrected row's own `occurred_on`, is not checked, so a purpose correction is never refused for a date she cannot edit: not for up to two hours after a change to a zone further west, and not on a legacy row dated ahead. The floor still applies, as a `CHECK`.
- **#624 and #625.** They land before #639 and write a default start month. They read it through `CurrentDuesPeriod`, not a new Asia/Jakarta literal, so #639 changes one seam.
- **Docs, in #639's PR.** PRD section 6 (Fund: "a name, a currency (IDR) and a time zone: WIB, WITA or WIT"), section 7.1 (first-run setup: "name the fund" gains "and choose its time zone"), `PRD-ID.md` (Dana: "nama, mata uang (IDR), dan zona waktu"; the same line in 7.1), `CONTEXT.md` (Fund names the field; one word, "time zone", `time_zone`, "zona waktu" in copy). `docs/Decisions.md` is the orchestrator's.
- **Superseded, and marked in #639's PR rather than now,** since the old clauses stay true until the code lands: [ADR-035](./035-public-report.md), "Current is reckoned in Asia/Jakarta, as the backups already are, never the server's clock" becomes the fund's zone; [ADR-037](./037-report-shows-the-month-as-it-ended.md), "the first instant of the next month in Asia/Jakarta" for the cek kas bound; [ADR-038](./038-the-month-as-a-balance-walk.md), `GET /api/balances` "in Asia/Jakarta". What each clause was *for* (never the server's clock) is kept; only which zone changes. [ADR-039](./039-e2e-every-operation-gated-in-ci.md)'s "02:00 Jakarta" is a CI schedule and unaffected. Partial supersession leaves their index rows at `implemented`.
- **Tests.** `CHECK` boundary tests through the API and through restore; a clock test per zone on CI's UTC clock (23:30 WIB on a month's last day is still that month for a WIB fund and already the next for a WIT fund, for the dues period, the report month and the picker bounds); a WITA and a WIT fund at 00:30 local can save the form's default date, including the first screen after load; a zone change leaves every row valid; the dump-name round trip across a zone change, in both directions; the Vitest that keeps `new Date()` inside `lib/dates.ts`; one e2e journey for Setup and Pengaturan with its `COVERAGE.md` row. The six e2e specs that compute today in Asia/Jakarta keep working: the seeded fund is WIB.
- **Accepted costs.** The list of zones lives in three places, two pinned by one Go test. The dump-name grammar gains an optional token, written only outside WIB. Operator files keep a WIB stamp. A zone change can move a boundary-straddling past cek kas into the neighbouring month's report. The `CHECK` that makes a bad zone unrepresentable also makes a wider list a rebuild of `fund` after `v1.0.0` ([ADR-025](./025-one-migration-file-until-1.0.md): an in-place edit before it).

## Scope check (PRD section 4)

This adds **one setting**, by the maintainer's decision, and nothing else new. It is none of the non-goals: no reminder, portal, gateway, journal, dashboard or second currency. It is the first fund-level setting beyond the name and the currency, but it is a fact about the community rather than a preference, and the closed list of three is the guard against it becoming a settings page: no locale, date format or week start follows from it. No new screen (a field on Setup's first step, a card in an existing section), no new dependency (`time/tzdata` is already embedded and browsers ship `Intl`), no new entity (CONTEXT.md names a field of Fund).

## Settled at approval (2026-10-20)

- **Setup pre-selects from the device's UTC offset**, falling back to WIB (above).
- **Pre-upgrade copy names stay Asia/Jakarta.** They are the operator's own files, read for rolling back and not for dating a day, and no fund can be read when they are written.
- **A WIB dump's name carries no token.** Only `utc8` and `utc9` are written, so both live instances' names, `SELF-HOSTING.md`'s examples, the image workflow's boot-dump check and the e2e filename assertion stay as they are.
- **The copy above is approved**, including the Pengaturan heading "Kas ini".
